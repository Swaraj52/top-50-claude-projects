Business operations hub

A process that only lives in one person's head is a risk, not a process.

Write the procedure once, run it as a checklist, and name the person who owns it — so the work still happens when the person who usually does it is away.

  • Library, not binders. Every SOP has a code, an owner, a review window and numbered steps.
  • Runs you can finish. A checklist is a snapshot of the steps, with progress and a due date.
  • Coverage you can see. The ownership matrix shows which areas and processes nobody holds.
